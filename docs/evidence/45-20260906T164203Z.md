## What `CLAIM-41.4` obliges, and what the skill owes it

Three obligations, all checked by `claim-lint` **after** the fact and all the
skill's job **before** it:

1. every Design claim appears in some case's `anchors_to`;
2. no case anchors to a claim that does not exist;
3. **every case declares a `Corpus`** — and the skill **refuses to emit a case
   table without the column.** Not a warning. Not a column added later.

### The Corpus column, and why `synthetic` is not a lesser value

`references/verification.md:90-115` is the contract; the body cites it rather
than restating the vocabulary.

The point worth carrying: **a plan that banned `synthetic` would delete the very
cases that prove an exemption is exact rather than a prefix** — the case class
this repository has needed six times. What is forbidden is an **undeclared or
unreasoned** synthetic corpus, so the reason belongs in the cell.

And the honest bound, stated in the body rather than papered over: the check can
verify a declaration **exists and is well formed**. It cannot verify it is
**true**. A `real` label can sit on invented fixtures and no regex will know.

## Two hazards specific to this verb

**Renumbering.** Case numbers are cited by evidence artifacts, by task issues and
by the Design's own coverage table. **Renumbering on a re-run silently reassigns
every one of those references** — the body forbids it in terms.

**Bidirectional invalidation.** This skill re-runs most often because a *gate
ruling changed the Design* — exactly when a stale plan is most dangerous. A
Design amendment invalidates a plan in **both** directions: a new claim leaves a
hole, a withdrawn claim leaves a dangling anchor. Both halves are read before
anything is written.
