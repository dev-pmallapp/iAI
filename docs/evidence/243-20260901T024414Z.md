## What makes the posture hard to reverse

Deleting a branch is not the same as preventing its return. Three things make
this one stick:

1. **`isConsentGranted` is deleted, not bypassed.** `checkEgressInternal` renames
   its third argument to `_consent` and never reads it. Re-enabling the path
   requires writing new code, not flipping a condition.
2. **Case 13 asserts deep equality** between the granted-consent and
   withheld-consent decisions across all four classes and both localities. That
   proves the parameter cannot influence anything, which is the property that
   makes retaining an inert parameter safe rather than merely tolerable.
3. **Case 11's test name states why it must not be re-enabled**, so a maintainer
   who breaks it reads the reason in the failure output rather than in a comment
   they never opened.

Decision 2 retained the consent mechanism, which is the more dangerous of the two
options — a threaded parameter that does nothing invites someone to wire it back
up. The obligation that came with retention is discharged by cases 11–14.
