## The linter and the matcher share one definition

`lint.ts` decides well-formedness by calling `matchSentinelLine` from
`sentinel.ts` rather than carrying a second regex. This is not a tidiness
preference: a producer that emits a shape the consumer cannot find is a defect
that would stay invisible until #28 builds the consumer, and would then present
as a consumer bug. Mutation 3 demonstrates the coupling — a change to the
matcher's boundary rule propagates to the linter's verdict.

## Multi-fault bodies report every fault

Confirmed by direct probe rather than assumed:

```
"```markdown\n## iai-design\n```\n"   -> ["not-fenced@2", "first-line@1"]
"  ## iai-evidence\n\nbody"          -> ["column-zero@1"]
"Here are the results.\n\n## iai..."  -> ["first-line@1"]
"## iai-design\n\n## iai-evidence"    -> ["one-per-comment@3"]
"  ### iai-design\n\nbody"            -> ["column-zero@1", "exact@1"]
"## iai-design\n\nbody"               -> []
```

A fenced sentinel reports **both** `not-fenced` and `first-line`, because both
are true — a fence opener necessarily occupies line 1. Suppressing the second
would hide a real fault to make a fixture tidier. An indented sentinel on line 1
correctly reports `column-zero` alone, with no spurious `first-line`, because a
candidate does occupy line 1.
