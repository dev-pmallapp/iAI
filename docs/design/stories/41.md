# S2.2 — Planning skills

**Story:** #41 · **Milestone:** M2 — Universal lifecycle
**Unit of work:** one Tier-1 skill
**Creates:** `skills/` — the first four `SKILL.md` files this repository has ever had
**Widens:** `scripts/skill-lint.ts` (two section rules, one domain rule), `packages/core/src/guards/path-allowlist.ts` (four retirements)
**Corrects:** `docs/milestones/M2.md` (five claims restated), `CONTRIBUTING.md` (a false statement about what the linter checks)

S2.1's difficulty was that four of its six claims governed a population it did
not create. **S2.2's is that five of its six seeded claims are false, ambiguous
or unimplementable at HEAD, and the sixth needs a harness that does not exist.**

That sounds like a complaint. It is not. Every one of the five was measured, and
three of them are the *same defect the last two issues fixed*, arriving a third
time from a different direction: **a rule written before anyone looked at the
corpus it governs.** #287 measured it, #289 made it a required declaration, and
this Story is where it stops being a lesson and becomes the reason the claims
are worded the way they are.

## Problem

**1. `CLAIM-41.3` would reject every Design in the repository, including the one
that defined the format.**

The seeded criterion requires `story-design` to write "all seventeen body
sections". Measured over `docs/design/stories/`, **denominator 9 of 9**:

| | |
|---|---|
| Designs with seventeen H2 sections | **0** |
| Maximum observed | **11** (`15.md`, `194.md`) |
| Modal and minimum | **10** (7 of 9 files) |
| Designs carrying YAML frontmatter at all | **1 of 9** (`9.md`, and it has *seven* keys, not the four at `references/design-format.md:31`) |

**The list of seventeen section names does not exist in this repository.** Every
one of the fourteen in-repo mentions restates the *number*; not one enumerates
the *names*. The only cited source is an upstream `ISAFormat.md` that is not
vendored here. **You cannot write a checker for a set whose members are unknown**
— this is not a rule that is merely unmet, it is a rule that cannot be
implemented.

And the repository already ruled against it. `docs/design/verification-pass.md:515`
(row 461) verdicts the "17 **fixed** sections" reading **`corrected`**, on the
ground that the upstream spec itself says *"Empty sections are excluded
entirely"*. `references/design-format.md:38-52` carries the disagreement forward
in terms, and `:145` names the owner: **`CLAIM-41.3`, S2.2**. It was routed here
deliberately. It bites here.

What the corpus actually shows is far more useful than the seeded rule: a
**ten-section ordered spine, identical in name and order in 9 of 9 files** —
`Problem → Vision → Out of Scope → Constraints → Dependencies → Goal → Claims →
Build Targets → Test Strategy → Decisions` — with exactly two additions in the
entire corpus (`## Mapping` at `194.md:57`, an `## Addendum` at `15.md:425`).

> **This is the third consecutive instance of one defect.** #280 capped
> references at 3 and rejected 7 of 9 real verbs. #289 found that a non-empty
> corpus is not a representative one. `CLAIM-41.3` would have been the third,
> and it is the worst of the three, because the rule's own subject —
> `docs/design/stories/35.md` — is the document that defined the contract being
> enforced.

**2. `CLAIM-41.6` bans an ordinary English word, and its carve-out exempts
nothing.**

The seeded criterion forbids a skill body containing `dev`, `trade`, `health`,
`wealth` or `know` "outside a `references/` citation path". Measured over the
twelve shipped reference documents:

| Token | Substring hits | Whole-word hits | False positives under a substring rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| `know` | **16** | 3 | **13** — `known` ×7, `knows` ×2, `knowledge` ×2, `unknown` ×2 |
| `dev` | 5 | 3 | 2 — `deviation` ×2 |
| `health` | 4 | 4 | 0 |
| `trade` | 2 | 2 | 0 |
| `wealth` | 1 | 1 | 0 |

`know` is an ordinary English word and **a whole-word rule does not save it** —
three legitimate uses of the verb survive, and two of the seven `known` hits are
markdown section headings (`## Known divergences`, `## Known drift`) that are a
house convention.

**The carve-out is zero-width.** None of the twelve reference *filenames*
contains any of the five tokens, so "outside a `references/` citation path"
exempts **0 of the 28** occurrences. The one token that *is* inside a citation
path — `dev` within `docs/design/04-domain-dev.md`, cited at
`references/context-discovery.md:73` — is not a `references/` path and so is not
carved out either.

**3. `CLAIM-41.5` and `CLAIM-41.6` are in direct contradiction.**

`CLAIM-41.5` requires each skill to resolve the binding from the `domain:` label
rather than assuming a domain. The doctrine it must implement is stated once, at
`references/domain-binding.md:13-14`:

> *"The kernel does not **know** what `trade` or `health` means — it **knows**
> how to read a binding."*

That single load-bearing sentence contains **four of the five banned tokens**.
So does the canonical statement of the rule itself at
`docs/design/01-skill-hierarchy.md:33` (*"There is no `if (domain === "trade")`
anywhere in Tier 1"*), and so does its sharpest form at `CONTRIBUTING.md:304`
(*"a Tier-1 verb may never **know** any"*). **Under `CLAIM-41.6` as seeded, the
four skills cannot state the rule `CLAIM-41.5` requires them to obey.** This is
`NEVER-35.7`'s bind — *a document whose subject is a constant may not contain
it* — reappearing one layer up.

**4. `goal-create`'s input does not exist in this repository, and its output
primitive does not exist at all.**

`CLAIM-41.1` requires a back-link comment "on the goal's `G0` line". Four
separate blockers, each fatal alone:

| | |
|---|---|
| The file | `USER/GOALS/GOALS.md` is a **gitignored symlink into a separate private repository**, absent on any public checkout (`.gitignore:6-8`, `references/data-classification.md:68`) |
| The format | **No `G0` line grammar exists anywhere** — no regex, no template, no parser. `ARCHITECTURE.md:174` names a format *family*; nothing specifies a line |
| The primitive | `packages/core/src/gh/comments.ts` exports three functions, all **issue-scoped**. There is no line-anchored comment builder anywhere in `packages/core/src` |
| The sentinel | the namespace is a **closed nine-name set** (`packages/core/src/evidence/sentinel.ts:22-44`); there is no goal back-link sentinel and a tenth name is a Design change |

The `intent/` module that `ARCHITECTURE.md:87` assigns "Goals parsing" to **does
not exist**. The milestone half of the claim, by contrast, is fully supported
today: `milestoneCreate`, `milestoneList` and `milestoneUpdate` all ship
(`packages/core/src/gh/milestones.ts:36-131`).

**5. `CLAIM-41.2` names an input that two design documents disagree about, and
is already false on disk.**

`docs/design/01-skill-hierarchy.md:93` says `story-create` reads the **milestone
description**. `docs/design/02-roles.md:137` says the planner parses
**`docs/milestones/M*.md`**. `references/context-discovery.md:35` sides with the
file. `docs/design/verification-pass.md:539` records this as an *"Internal
contradiction"* — and that register's rows are **immutable** by Decision 9 of
`docs/design/stories/35.md`, so it must be resolved forward, not edited away.

Worse, the claim's arithmetic is already wrong. **`docs/milestones/M1.md` has
five feature rows and six `### S` headings** — `S1.6` has no feature row.
"Exactly one Story per feature-table row" is false against the repository's own
history.

And the job is half-done by something else. `scripts/bootstrap-stories.py`
already created **36 Stories and 142 tasks**, keyed on `### S{m}.{n}` headings
rather than feature rows, idempotent by exact issue title, and it also does
`task-create`'s job. It is positioned as a temporary stand-in
(`scripts/bootstrap-github.sh:526-527`). **Nothing in this repository has ever
parsed a `| Feature | Description |` table.**

**6. "Runs twice and makes zero mutations" cannot be verified by anything that
exists.**

`docs/milestones/M2.md:107-109` sets the verification as an idempotency re-run.
A skill is **markdown interpreted by an LLM host**. There is no harness that can
execute one: no host runner, no LLM call, no fixture repository, no recording
proxy anywhere in `packages/`, `scripts/`, `test/` or `.github/workflows/`.
`packages/core/src/gh/*` is pure argv construction by design and never executes.
The milestone lists the harness as a task of *this* Story
(`docs/milestones/M2.md:105`), so it is work to be done, not a tool to be used.

**7. `CONTRIBUTING.md:128-129` is false, and nothing can see that it is false.**

It states that `skill-lint` checks *"description length under 300 characters, a
Phase 0 context-discovery section, and an Error Handling section."* **Two of the
three do not exist.** The complete rule set is fourteen ids at
`scripts/skill-lint.ts:36-55`; none inspects a heading.

The requirement is real — `CONTRIBUTING.md:272-275` and `:280-282` — and
entirely unenforced. It survived because **`skills/` is outside `claim-lint`'s
scope** (`scripts/claim-lint.ts:36`), so nothing cross-checks CONTRIBUTING's
description of the linter against the linter.

**8. The four new files land in a near-zero-coverage zone.**

`claim-lint` cannot see `skills/` at all: a malformed `CLAIM-` identifier, a
dangling `anchors_to`, or a dangling path citation inside a `SKILL.md` is
completely unchecked. `skill-lint` checks frontmatter and three body rules and
nothing else. This is the `skill-lint`-green-over-zero-files weakness inverted:
the linter stops being vacuous this Story, and the *other* linter's blind spot
opens at the same moment.

**9. `CONTRIBUTING.md:147` instructs a contributor to run a file that does not
exist.** `packages/installer/src/cli.ts` is absent; the whole installer package
is a ten-line `describeInstall` returning a string. `CONTRIBUTING.md:286-287`
tells the author of a new skill to run `install-dry` and confirm the skill loads
on both hosts. **S2.2 is the first Story whose author will actually try that**,
and the first to discover it is not runnable. The behaviour belongs to M8.

## Vision

Four markdown files that turn intent into pre-registered, machine-checkable
claims — and, more importantly, **the first four files that prove the linting
layer built across M1 and S2.1 was worth building.**

Every rule S2.1 shipped has been green over zero skills. This Story supplies the
denominator. The three body rules get a real population, the frontmatter schema
stops being theoretical, and `skill-lint: 0 SKILL.md files scanned` — this
repository's standing example of a check that passes while checking nothing —
**stops being true for the first time since S1.3.**

The claims below are worded against what the corpus actually contains, not
against what the milestone assumed it would. That is the whole of the method
being adopted here, and it is now enforced: #289's `Corpus` column means the
plan for this Story must declare, case by case, whether it is testing the real
thing or a fixture of it.

## Decisions

### Decision 1 — `goal-create`'s back-link is severed, and the milestone half ships

> **RULED YES at the gate (G3), 2026-09-06. Raised as #292.**

The back-link requires a file in another repository, a line grammar that is
nowhere specified, a `gh` primitive that does not exist, and a tenth sentinel in
a closed set. Four blockers, any one fatal.

**The claim is restated to the half that is real.** `milestoneCreate`,
`milestoneList` and `milestoneUpdate` all ship and are tested; idempotency by
milestone title is verifiable today.

The severed half is **recorded, not deleted**. It is **#292**, which must answer
whether iAI ships `templates/GOALS.template.md` (`docs/design/09-security.md:300`
specifies it; neither it nor `templates/` exists) — because without a public
fixture any Goals rule would ship green over a corpus of zero, which is the exact
failure #289 was filed about.

> **A naming drift found while measuring this, and not resolved here.** iAI
> specifies `USER/GOALS/GOALS.md`; the ancestor repository uses
> `USER/TELOS/TELOS.md`, and `docs/design/verification-pass.md:204` verifies the
> claim against the *ancestor* path. No rename decision is recorded anywhere; it
> has only ever been inherited. **Owner: #292.**

### Decision 2 — the milestone document on disk is authoritative, and the identity key is the feature row

Two design documents disagree and the register that recorded the disagreement is
immutable. Resolving forward:

**`docs/milestones/M*.md` on disk is the input.** Three reasons, in order of
weight: `references/context-discovery.md:35` — a *contract document*, and the
one skills are required to read — already names it as the parse target; the
GitHub milestone description is a **truncated copy** of it
(`scripts/bootstrap-github.sh:44`, budget 10,000 chars); and a file in the
working tree is re-readable, diffable and testable, while a milestone
description is not.

**The identity key is the feature-row description text**, not the `S{m}.{n}` id.
This is what makes the claim "one Story per row" mean anything.

**Consequence, stated rather than discovered later:** run against M1 this finds
**five rows and six existing Stories**, because `S1.6` has no feature row.
`story-create` must report the orphan and create nothing, not silently
reconcile. A skill that quietly papers over a real inconsistency between the
milestone and the issue tracker is worse than one that stops.

**`scripts/bootstrap-stories.py` coexists and is not retired here.** It keys on
`### S` headings, spans two verbs' jobs, and created thirty-six issues whose
titles `story-create` must not duplicate. Retirement needs those issues
reconciled; that is not this Story's unit of work.

### Decision 3 — the ten-section ordered spine replaces "all seventeen"

> **RULED YES at the gate, both parts (G1a and G1b), 2026-09-06.**

The seeded rule cannot be implemented — the seventeen names do not exist in this
repository — and would reject 9 of 9 shipped Designs if they did.

**The rule becomes: a Design carries the ten-section spine, in relative order.**

`Problem · Vision · Out of Scope · Constraints · Dependencies · Goal · Claims ·
Build Targets · Test Strategy · Decisions`

Measured, not chosen: identical in name and order in **9 of 9** files, with
`## Mapping` and `## Addendum` as the only additions in the entire corpus.
Additional sections are permitted; the spine must be present and ordered.
`references/design-format.md:52` already defines conformance as an **ordering**
property and never as a membership one, so this is the reference document's own
rule made checkable.

Authority: `docs/design/verification-pass.md:515` verdicts the "17 fixed"
reading `corrected`. `references/design-format.md:145` names `CLAIM-41.3` as the
owner. **This Design is itself written to the spine**, so the rule's first
positive fixture is the document that declares it.

**Frontmatter is restated too — G1b, ruled separately and also yes.** 8 of 9
Designs have none; the one that does carries seven keys, not the four specified,
and its values are stale (`phase: scoping`, `progress: 0/9` on a Story that
shipped). The convention 8 of 9 follow — an **H1 title plus a bolded metadata
block** (`**Story:**`, `**Milestone:**`, `**Unit of work:**`, `**Creates:**`) —
is what the rule describes. `references/design-format.md:29-34` carries the
correction, which is Build Target 10.

Both halves were put separately because they fail for different reasons: the
section half is *unimplementable* (the names do not exist), the frontmatter half
is *contradicted by the corpus* (8 of 9 do something else). Ruling them together
without saying so would have hidden that.

### Decision 4 — the domain rule bans the routing form, not the word

> **RULED YES at the gate (G2), 2026-09-06**, including dropping `know` entirely.

Banning the bare tokens is unimplementable (`know` appears 16 times in
`references/`, 13 of them ordinary English) and self-contradictory (the doctrine
the skills must state contains four of the five tokens).

**The rule bans `domain:<id>` and `skills/<id>/` for any id in
`KNOWN_DOMAIN_IDS`.** Real-roster hits across the twelve references: **zero**.

This is the right target on the merits, not merely the tractable one. What
`CLAIM-41.5` protects against is a skill that **hardcodes a routing decision** —
`if (domain === "trade")`. It does not protect against a skill that explains, in
prose, that it must not do so. The shape matches `no-domain-pack-import`
(`scripts/lint.ts:449`), which bans an import specifier rather than a word, and
it inherits `model-id-literal`'s lesson: a generic pattern *"would flag every
document in the tree"* (`scripts/skill-lint.ts:454-457`).

**The rule imports `KNOWN_DOMAIN_IDS` from `packages/core/src/binding/domain.ts:73`.**
Restating five literals would be a `duplicate-contract` violation and would
break the open-union design — `DomainId` is deliberately open, so a hardcoded
five-token rule would need editing to add a sixth domain, which is the precise
failure `ARCHITECTURE.md:209` names.

> `know` is dropped from the ban entirely, and the count is the reason:
> 16 substring occurrences, 3 whole-word, 13 false positives. Recorded so that
> nobody restores it believing it was an oversight.

### Decision 5 — the two missing section rules ship, rather than the sentence being softened

`CONTRIBUTING.md:128-129` claims `skill-lint` checks a Phase 0
context-discovery section and an Error Handling section. Neither rule exists.

Two ways to make them agree. **The rules ship.** The requirement is real
(`CONTRIBUTING.md:272-275`, `:280-282`), it is exactly the kind of structural
property a linter can check, and this is the first Story with a population to
check it over. Deleting the sentence would discharge the contradiction by
lowering the standard, which is the opposite of what four Stories of enforcement
work have been for.

**And the reason it survived is the more important finding:** `skills/` is
outside `claim-lint`'s scope, so nothing cross-checked CONTRIBUTING's
description of the linter against the linter. Build Target 7 asks whether
`skills/` should enter `SCOPE_DIRS`. If the answer is no, the exclusion gets a
recorded reason, because "nobody thought about it" is how this one survived.

### Decision 6 — idempotency is verified as a stated contract, not as an execution

> **RULED YES at the gate (G4), 2026-09-06. The harness is #293, sized L, on M2.**

No harness can run a skill: no host runner, no LLM call, no fixture repository,
nothing. The milestone's re-run verification describes a capability that does
not exist and that this Story would have to build from nothing.

**`CLAIM-41.8` is restated to a structural property that is real**: each skill
declares its re-entry condition, and every mutating step it describes is
preceded by the read that makes it idempotent. That is checkable by review over
four documents, and it is honest about being `model-judged` rather than dressed
up as `tool-checked` — which `references/verification.md:45-46` names as the
failure the taxonomy exists to prevent.

**The re-run harness is not descoped into silence.** It is **#293**, sized **L**
at the gate, on M2: a fixture repository, a host runner and a mutation recorder,
three build targets and none of them a planning skill.

That Story must first decide something this one only names: **a live-model
harness and a replay harness verify different things, and only one of them
verifies idempotency.** A replayed transcript proves the *recording* is stable;
it cannot detect that a skill would issue a second `gh issue create` on a real
second run, because the recording never made the first one. S2.3, S2.4 and S2.5
inherit `CLAIM-41.8`'s restatement until #293 lands.

### What the ruling settles beyond this Story

**A design document that defines a rule must be able to satisfy it.** G2's
supporting evidence was that `docs/design/01-skill-hierarchy.md:33` — the
canonical statement of domain-agnosticism, *"There is no `if (domain ===
"trade")` anywhere in Tier 1"* — contains the banned token, as does
`CONTRIBUTING.md:304`, which uses `know` twice to say a Tier-1 verb may never
know a domain. Under the seeded rule the two documents that define the rule
violate it. Under the restated rule both are fine, because they are prose rather
than routing forms.

That is the same shape as `NEVER-35.7`'s bind — *a document whose subject is a
constant may not contain it* — and the same resolution: **ban the form that does
the harm, not the word that describes it.** Third occurrence; worth naming.
