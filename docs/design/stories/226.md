## Problem

#14 verdicted every assertion in `docs/design/` and, in doing so, produced three
findings it was not chartered to fix. Each was filed and then stranded.

**1. A milestone described a linter that was never built.**
`docs/milestones/M1.md` scoped the frontmatter linter to "the three docs
targets". `scripts/skill-lint.ts` collects only files named `SKILL.md`, matching
the design, `CONTRIBUTING.md` and `.github/workflows/ci.yml`. The linter was
right and the milestone was the outlier — but a test case had already been
derived from the milestone's wording, and that case could not be made to execute
in either direction.

**2. A resolved risk was still recorded as open.** Row 383 of
`docs/design/verification-pass.md` recorded that "the last matching rule wins in
opencode" could not be substantiated, and drew the consequence: if precedence
were first-match or most-specific-match, every generated agent's deny rules would
be inert, including the `.env` control. That is a shipped-behaviour risk, and it
sat in §4.3 unresolved because the pass's three sources do not contain the
answer. The shipped binary does.

**3. The pass misreported its own size, and disagreed with itself about it.**
`verification-pass.md` presents one total, **807**, where two different totals
are true. Separately, §5's prose contradicted the table three lines above it,
carrying `original` at 231 against a table reading 227, and `corrected` at 147
against 152.

**The root cause of the stranding is procedural, not editorial.** All three were
filed as `type:task` against Story #9, which closed. `CONTRIBUTING.md` gives a
task branch exactly one destination — its Story branch — so once #9's branch was
deleted there was no in-bounds route for any of them. They were not blocked on
judgement; they were blocked on having nowhere to land.

## Vision

M1's design layer states only what M1 shipped. Where the pass recorded a doubt
that has since been resolved, the record says so and names how it was resolved.
Where the pass reported a number, that number reconciles against the pass's own
tables.

## Constraints

- **Verdict cells are immutable.** The supersession rule that made
  `verification-pass.md` an allow-listed path during #194 applies here in full.
  An annotation may be rewritten; the verdict beside it may not.
- **`docs/evidence/` is immutable, and so is every posted sentinel comment.**
  #14's `## iai-evidence` comment carries the superseded 807 reading. It records
  what was reported at the time and stays as posted. Forward-only.
- **Case numbers are never reassigned.** `docs/evidence/` cites test cases by
  number. Renumbering to close a gap would silently invalidate those citations.
- **Counts are re-derived, never transcribed.** A cell in this pass may contain
  `|` inside a code span or as `\|`, and the two table shapes place the verdict
  column at different indices. Any recount anchors on each table's own header.

### Decision 2 — case 22 is retired, not restated

Case 22 of `docs/test-plans/9-plan.md` asserted two things: that a `skill-lint`
rejection **on a docs target** can be observed, and that the resulting fix
correlates to a `corrected` row citing its commit.

The second half became satisfiable when the pass shipped. The first never was,
and #197's correction removes the wording it was derived from. Restating the case
down to its working half was available and was rejected: it would have invented a
new test case under an old number, and #197 is explicit that making case 22
executable was not the intent. Both claims it anchored keep their coverage from
other cases, so retirement costs nothing.

The number is left unallocated. Two evidence files cite case numbers in this
plan, and closing the gap by renumbering would invalidate them silently — the
same forward-only rule that governs the evidence itself.

### Decision 3 — both 807 and 783 are kept

#198 recorded that the headline 807 was overstated by 24 and attributed the
excess to §4.1 being counted twice. An independent recount, anchored on each
table's own header, does not support the attribution:

- the `Rows` column of the §5 table sums to **807**, exactly;
- the four verdict columns sum to **783**, exactly;
- every per-section figure reconciles, with zero blank verdicts and no duplicate
  row ids.

The 24-row difference is **§2, the cross-document conflict register**, whose rows
record internal contradictions rather than source reconciliations and therefore
carry no verdict. The document already said as much. §4.1 also holds 24 entries,
which is a coincidence: it indexes the false-provenance rows already counted in
§3.

Both figures are therefore correct and neither is overstated. Setting the total
to 783 as filed would have left the `Rows` column no longer summing — replacing a
wording defect with an arithmetic one. The correction states the distinction
instead: 807 is the size of the pass, 783 is the number of verdicted assertions.

This departs from a written `Done when`, and was put to @dev-pmallapp and
approved before being written. The corrected diagnosis is recorded on #198 so
that the issue's own recount table does not mislead a later reader.
